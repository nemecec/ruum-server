Ruum brand directions

Three directions for the look of Ruum, for you to choose one. Each has a character, a light and a dark palette (every text pair passes WCAG 2.2 AA), a type pairing, an icon style, a wordmark sketch and the same three screens: the file browser with an agent's change, the share dialog and the agents page. The words on the screens follow the voice of docs/ux/copy.md: informal and playful, calm where data, security or money are at stake.

Nothing here changes the product. A chosen direction is applied later through the token files only; docs/ux/brand/README.md says which tokens change. The mock screens are drawings, not the product; the sizes are compact so that light and dark fit side by side.

1. Paper and spruceWarm paper, deep spruce, a soft serif. Calm first; the jokes live in the words.
2. MarkerInk, off-white and a yellow highlighter. The playful voice, made visible.
3. GraphiteCool graphite, fjord blue, square corners and mono. Exact and quiet.
Direction 1

Paper and spruce

The quiet notebook

Ruum looks like a well-kept notebook on a wooden desk. Warm paper neutrals, a deep spruce green for the one thing to do next, and a clay colour that belongs only to agents. Titles are set in Fraunces, a soft serif with round, slightly wobbly ends, so every page has a human hand in it; the body text stays in the system font, so Ruum feels at home on every device and reads well in Estonian, German or Polish. Nothing shouts. The playful voice does the smiling, and the visuals keep the promise that your work is safe. This is the closest to the tokens Ruum has today: choosing it changes no colour at all.

Wordmark and app icon

Lowercase "ruum" as one even, round line, like a word written with a good pen. The two u's stand close together: a room with two people in it.

Direction 2

Marker

The highlighter

Ruum looks like the wall of a lively studio: black ink on off-white paper, and a yellow highlighter on the one thing that matters on this screen (the selected row, the main note, the wordmark). Titles are set in Bricolage Grotesque, a bold grotesque with a bit of mischief; the body is Figtree, round and open. Buttons are chunky, with a hard ink edge. Agents wear cobalt, a colour nothing else uses, so their work stands out at a glance. This direction carries the informal voice into the pictures. Where data, security or money are at stake, the yellow and the hard edges step back and plain ink remains.

Wordmark and app icon

"Ruum" in heavy round strokes, with a quick yellow highlighter swipe behind the "uu". It is the only place where the brand is allowed to be a little loud.

Direction 3

Graphite

The precise instrument

Ruum looks like a well-made instrument for people who care about the exact state of things. Cool graphite neutrals, a deep fjord blue for actions, square corners and thin rules. IBM Plex Sans carries the text and IBM Plex Mono shows sizes, times and key prefixes, so numbers line up and read like facts. Agents are marked in a raspberry plum that appears nowhere else. There is no decoration at all: the playfulness lives only in the copy. It is the calmest of the three and the most at home next to developer tools, and the furthest from the warm voice.

Wordmark and app icon

A rectilinear "ruum" built from straight strokes, held in four corner brackets: the outline of a room, or a selection on a screen.

How to choose

Ask three questions, in this order.

  1. Who should feel at home first? Small studios and offices that keep their work in Ruum (Paper and spruce, Marker), or developers and IT people who connect agents to it (Graphite).
  2. Where should the playfulness live? Only in the words (Paper and spruce, Graphite), or in the words and the pictures (Marker).
  3. How much change now? Paper and spruce changes no colour and adds one self-hosted font (Fraunces, for titles). Marker and Graphite change every colour token and add two self-hosted font families.

My recommendation: 1, Paper and spruce.

Ruum's promise is "a friendly colleague who also keeps your work safe". The voice already carries the friendly part, and it does it well. The visuals should carry the safe part: warm, quiet, and the same on every screen, so a security or delete dialog never looks like a joke. Paper and spruce does that, keeps the agents' clay colour that people already learn as "an agent did this", passes every contrast pair today, and needs no colour change. The soft serif in titles and the round wordmark add the character that the current system font lacks.

Choose Marker if you want Ruum to be recognisable from across the room and the playful voice to show before anyone reads a word; it costs more care on calm screens (the yellow must stay off errors, security and billing). Choose Graphite if the first buyers are developers and IT, and you accept a look that is cooler than the voice.
